When you expand from apps and extensions into Merch on Demand, the first question reviewers ask — explicitly or not — is: *whose IP is this actually for?*

Here's the process we use to answer that cleanly, every time.

## Start with what you own

Every design we publish is tied to a product BramLabs owns. The starting point is never a trend, a meme template, or a licensed character. It's a product mark: a wordmark, an icon system, or a motif we built from scratch for one of our products.

For example, the **StoryPeek mark** uses an eye/peek geometry we designed specifically for the extension. It references what the tool does without borrowing any platform's visual language. The same principle applies to SnapElite's capture-grid motif and SonicShield's waveform lockup.

## Build at print specs from the start

We work at 300 DPI from the first frame — not for screen and then scaled up. The practical difference:

- **300 DPI at export size**: Lines stay crisp at garment print scale. Thin type doesn't feather.
- **Transparent PNG master**: Amazon's garment workflow needs the design on a transparent background. Exporting from a white canvas is a common mistake that kills contrast on coloured shirts.
- **RGB working files**: Print suppliers handle their own colour conversions. Delivering RGB with solid fills (not gradients that rely on screen gamut) gives us predictable output.

Safe zone is 0.25 in from all garment edges. We check this in the preview step, not after submitting.

## Run USPTO trademark screening

Before finalising any wordmark or distinctive mark, we run a USPTO TESS search on:

- The exact product name
- Any abbreviations or initials used in the design
- Any distinctive taglines

If there's a collision — anything in an active or pending status in a remotely related class — we either redesign around it or drop the text element entirely. One search session per design is not optional; it's the step that separates a compliant publish from a takedown.

## Final policy pass

The last check before upload is a full read of the Amazon Content Policy for the design. The things we look for:

- No celebrity names or likenesses
- No references to sports teams or organisations
- No slogans associated with existing brands (even if the slogans aren't trademarked)
- No content that qualifies as misleading or deceptive

If the design passes all four checks, it publishes under the BramLabs merch account. If anything feels uncertain, it goes back for redesign — not into the queue hoping it slips through.

## Why this matters

The short answer: a rejection or takedown costs more time than the screening does. One suspension can affect your entire account. Doing the checks upfront is just basic risk management.

The longer answer: original product merch is a genuinely good business extension for indie developers. Your users already know the product name. A well-designed tee or hoodie with your mark on it closes a loop between digital product and physical ownership. That only works if the designs are yours.
