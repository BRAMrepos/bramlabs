The web has drifted toward trackers and retention loops. Browser extensions still sit in a useful middle layer: they can change how a page behaves without asking the user to leave their workflow.

That power is easy to abuse. Plenty of extensions request `<all_urls>`, scrape history, and sell data. Users learn to distrust the whole category.

## How BramLabs approaches permissions

We treat permissions as part of the product:

- Request only what the feature needs.
- Prefer client-side processing when possible.
- Spell out what we access on the product privacy page.

**Tab Snoozer**, **SnapElite**, and **SonicShield** each have their own privacy, terms, help, and support pages on this site for that reason.

## Where extensions fit in the studio

Extensions are one lane next to Amazon Appstore apps, web products, and original merch. The same rule applies across all of them: ship something concrete, own the brand, and do not lean on third-party IP.
